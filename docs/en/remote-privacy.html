Privacy Policy

StickyPresenter Remote Controller (iOS) · Last updated: October 17, 2026

Summary

Remote Controller has no accounts, no ads, and no tracking, and it does not collect personal information. Your timers and commands travel only between your iPhone and a Mac running StickyPresenter on your own local network. Apart from that, the app sends only anonymous usage statistics, crash reports, and the feedback you choose to send — all through Apple's iCloud (CloudKit), read only by the developer.

Data We Collect

We do not collect your name, contact details, location, advertising identifiers, or the content of your timers. We receive only the anonymous statistics and crash reports below, and feedback you send yourself.

Anonymous Usage Statistics

To learn how many people use the app and which versions are still in use, the app sends:

  • a random ID created when the app is installed (not linked to you, your Apple Account, your iPhone, or any other app; it changes if you reinstall)
  • app version, iOS version, device type, and region/language setting
  • how many times the app was opened, the days it was opened, the install date, and how many days have passed since then

Statistics are sent only if you are signed in to iCloud.

Crash Reports

When the app crashes or freezes, iOS gives it a diagnostic report (Apple's MetricKit), usually about once a day. The app forwards the call stack, app version, iOS version, and device type. No identifier — not even the random ID above — is attached.

Feedback

If you send feedback from Settings → Feedback, we receive what you write, any contact details you choose to add, and the app version, iOS version, and device model so we can reproduce the problem. If iCloud is unavailable, the app offers to send it by email instead.

What Stays on Your iPhone

To reconnect without asking for the code again, the app keeps two values on your iPhone: a random identifier created for this installation, and the identifier of the Mac you paired with. They are not tied to you or your Apple Account, are shared only with your paired Mac, and are removed when you unpair or delete the app.

Local Network

The app's only purpose is to control timers on your Mac, so it needs the Local Network permission. It uses Apple's MultipeerConnectivity framework to discover and connect to Macs on the same network that advertise the StickyPresenter timer service (_sp-timer). The connection is direct between your iPhone and your Mac and is encrypted. If you decline the permission, the app cannot find your Mac and nothing else in it will work.

What Is Exchanged

Only what is needed to draw the remote screen and act on your taps:

  • Timer information — name, remaining and target time, running / finished / hidden state, Pomodoro phase and cycle, widget size, theme, and the widget's position within your Mac's screen layout.
  • Your commands — start, pause, reset, add or subtract 30 seconds, resize, move, hide, align, delete, and create a preset timer.
  • Device names — as part of discovery, your iPhone's device name is visible to the Mac and the Mac's computer name is shown in the app. This is how Apple's MultipeerConnectivity identifies peers, and it is visible to devices on the same network that look for this service.

Your sticky notes and teleprompter scripts are never sent to the iPhone.

Who Can Connect

Connecting requires a four-digit pairing code. The code is shown on the Mac under menu bar timer icon → Remote, and you enter it once in this app the first time you connect. Once it matches, the Mac remembers that iPhone and later connections need no code.

A device that does not know the code cannot connect, even on the same local network. This app only lists the Macs it finds nearby, and exchanges data with the single Mac it is paired with. On the Mac you can draw a fresh code with New Pairing Code or clear every remembered device with Forget Paired Remotes; in this app you can tap the connection indicator to unpair. Only the timer information above is exchanged, and only timers can be controlled. Quit StickyPresenter on the Mac to stop it from being discoverable.

Internet, Ads, Tracking

Apart from the statistics, crash reports, and feedback above, and reading a small on/off settings record that lets us turn off a broken feature without an app update, the app makes no internet requests. It contains no advertising, no tracking, and no third-party SDKs, and it does not use the App Tracking Transparency framework because it has nothing to track. We do not sell or share your information with anyone.

Children

The app does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children.
